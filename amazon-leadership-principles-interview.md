# How to Prepare for Amazon's Leadership Principles Interview

Amazon interviews are unusual: almost every round, including technical ones, includes behavioral questions tied to the company's 16 Leadership Principles. Candidates who prepare only for coding often stumble here. This guide shows how to build answers that hold up to Amazon's follow-up questions.

## 1. Know what the interviewers are scoring

Each interviewer is usually assigned two or three principles to assess. They ask a question, then dig with follow-ups such as "What exactly did you do?", "What data did you use?" and "What would you do differently?" They write down your answer almost word for word and score it against the principle. That means vague, team-level answers ("we decided to...") score poorly. Use "I" and be specific.

## 2. Map stories to principles, not questions

You cannot predict every question, but you can prepare 8 to 10 strong stories and know which principles each one demonstrates. The principles that come up most often:

- **Customer Obsession**: a time you went beyond the request to solve a customer's real problem.
- **Ownership**: a time you took on something outside your job because it mattered.
- **Dive Deep**: a time you found the root cause by digging into data or logs.
- **Bias for Action**: a time you made a decision quickly with incomplete information.
- **Deliver Results**: a time you hit a hard goal despite setbacks.
- **Earn Trust**: a time you admitted a mistake or gave difficult feedback.
- **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit**: a time you pushed back, then committed to the final decision.
- **Learn and Be Curious**: a time you taught yourself something new to get a job done.

Most good stories cover two or three principles, so a list of 8 to 10 is usually enough for a full loop.

## 3. Use STAR, with numbers and trade-offs

Structure each story as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the setup to about 20 seconds and spend most of your time on your actions and reasoning. End with a measurable result (latency down 40%, churn down 3 points, launch two weeks early) and one sentence on what you learned.

Amazon interviewers like trade-offs. Mention what you chose not to do and why. It shows judgment, which is what "Are Right, A Lot" is really about.

## 4. Prepare for the follow-up chain

Practice each story with at least three follow-ups:

1. "What was the hardest part, and how did you handle it?"
2. "How did you measure success?"
3. "If you did it again, what would you change?"

If a story falls apart after two follow-ups, it is too thin. Pick another one or add detail.

## 5. Have a failure story ready

You will almost certainly be asked about a failure or a mistake. Choose a real one with real consequences, own it without blaming others, and spend most of the answer on what you changed afterward.

## 6. Rehearse out loud

Write bullet points, not scripts, then say each answer out loud and time it at two to three minutes. Mock interviews with a friend who interrupts with follow-ups are the best practice. Disclosure: I work on Linkjob AI, which offers [real-time interview help](https://www.linkjob.ai/) and practice based on your resume, if you want another way to rehearse.

## Quick checklist

- 8 to 10 stories, each mapped to two or three principles
- Every story uses "I", has a number, and mentions a trade-off
- Each story survives three follow-up questions
- One honest failure story
- Two or three thoughtful questions for each interviewer

Prepare this way and the behavioral rounds become the most predictable part of the Amazon loop.
